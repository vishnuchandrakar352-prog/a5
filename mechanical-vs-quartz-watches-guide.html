Mechanical vs Quartz Watches

Both tell the time, but they do it in completely different ways. One relies on springs and tiny gears; the other on a vibrating crystal and a battery.

00:12

How quartz works

A quartz watch uses a battery to make a tiny crystal vibrate exactly 32,768 times a second. A circuit counts those vibrations and sends a pulse to a small motor once a second, which is why the seconds hand usually jumps.

00:24

How mechanical works

A mechanical watch has no battery. A wound spring drives gears and a balance wheel, ticking several times a second. That is why the seconds hand seems to sweep in small, fast steps.

00:36

Accuracy

  • Quartz: often within 15 seconds a month.
  • Good mechanical: within a few seconds a day.
  • Certified chronometers: typically within −4 to +6 seconds a day.
00:48

Care and servicing

A quartz watch needs a new battery every few years and little else. A mechanical watch should be serviced every five to ten years, when it is taken apart, cleaned, oiled and adjusted. With care, it can run for generations.

00:60

Which to choose?

Choose quartz for low cost, accuracy and convenience. Choose mechanical if you enjoy craftsmanship, the sweeping seconds hand and a watch that runs on your own movement. Many collectors happily own both.
